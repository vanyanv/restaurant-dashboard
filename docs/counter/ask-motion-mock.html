The palette does not animate open. The answer swaps in without leaving.

⌘K is pressed dozens of times a day, so it opens instantly — no scale, no fade. What earns motion is the moment the list becomes an answer: a 160ms crossfade with a 2px blur so two states never overlap visibly. Escape or "Back to search" returns you with the question still in the box.

Why it moves

Spatial consistency: the answer replaces the list in the same frame, so it reads as the palette answering, not as a second window.

Change the week on the answer, not in a new question

Every answer carries the store and range it was computed for as chips. Tap the range and the same tools re-run; the figures count from the old value to the new one, the gap bar retargets its widths, and the chart's bars move to their new heights. The reader watches the number change rather than reloading the page in their head.

Why it moves

Explanation. A transition between two values shows the direction of change; a cut shows two unrelated numbers. Uses transitions, not keyframes, so a second tap mid-flight retargets smoothly.

An answer names what it read, and the reader can check it in place

Each source chip is verified (green mark) because it is one of the 58 curated loaders, and it carries its sync time — the one with the amber dot is the open range that will move at the next sync. "How I got here" opens a drawer with the exact calls and parameters. Why it moves

Feedback and origin. The drawer grows from its toggle (grid-rows, 220ms). The reason popover scales from the thumb it belongs to (bottom-right origin, .96→1, 150ms), then morphs into a "Noted" line rather than vanishing.

A refusal is an answer. A partial answer says what it could still read.

Recipe costs time out. The Monday answer is on the phone before the owner is

A saved question ran after the 06:00 Otter sync. Its card is the first thing on the phone home: the question, the verdict, the two figures, the delta since last Monday, and "Open in Ask" to carry on. Below it, the prior-question recall. The mic is for a ten-word question in a kitchen — hold to speak, release to send; it transcribes server-side so it works on every iOS browser.

Why it moves

Rare, so it may have a little delight: the card enters once, 340ms, when the sync lands. The mic's ring is constant-motion feedback that it is listening; it stops the moment you let go.
