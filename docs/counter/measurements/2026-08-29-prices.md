# Price monitor — measured before building

`P.prices`, `/dashboard/ingredients/prices`. Measured 2026-08-29.

---

## The prototype's central idea is right, and its numbers are backwards

`P.prices`'s note is **"Every price that moved, ranked by what the move
actually costs you"**, and its table header says *"Ranked by what it costs
you, not by percentage"*. That is the correct design and this page keeps it.

Its strip claims **Basket ▲9.4%**, **Worst mover ▲31% chicken thigh**, **Cost
of the moves $1,284**. Measured over 44 ingredients with at least three priced
deliveries in 120 days:

**The basket is down, not up. Net −$617 over 30 days at current volume.**

The largest single move by money is the fry, **down 15.5%**, saving $860.

## Which window you compare decides what you report

Two formulations of "the move", same data:

| method | fries reads | cost |
|---|---|---:|
| median of last 30d vs median of prior 30d | **+67.0%** | +$3,150 |
| latest price vs its own trailing median | **−15.5%** | −$860 |

The fry price oscillates between exactly $1.0370/lb and $1.7315/lb on an
identical pack shape (6 × 4.5 lb, 37 deliveries):

```
Jul 18–25   $1.0370   ×5 deliveries
Jul 27–Aug 17  $1.7315   ×10 deliveries
Aug 20–27   $1.0370   ×4 deliveries   ← where it is now
```

A 30-versus-30 comparison straddles that step and reports a 67% rise, at a
moment when the price has come all the way back down. **The window is the
artefact, not the data.** The page compares the latest delivery against the
trailing median of that ingredient's own history, which is what "what does it
cost me now" means.

## Ranking by percentage surfaces a parsing bug, not a price

The largest percentage move in the data is **`keyston sanitizer multi quat
liq`, +1,004.9%** — $4.60/gal to $50.88/gal. It is not a price move:

```
Jul 18   $96.69   q=1 CS   pack=1   size=21 GAL   → 21 gal   $4.6043/gal
Aug 01   $96.69   q=1 CS   pack=21  size=1 GAL    → 21 gal   $4.6043/gal
Aug 06  $101.75   q=1 CS   pack=2   size=1 GAL    →  2 gal  $50.8750/gal
```

Same case, near-identical invoice price, **pack read as 2 instead of 21**.
This is the pack-metadata mis-parse family that already has a guard —
`COST_SPIKE_THRESHOLD = 8` in `src/lib/invoice-line-shape.ts`. It catches this
one (11× the median), and the page states what it held out rather than
silently dropping it.

**9 of the 44 ingredients have more than one pack shape recorded against a
single SKU.** Those are marked, not excluded: they are usually a genuine pack
change, and only the ones that also breach the 8× guard are held out.

and costs $41; the ground beef moved **1.2%** and costs $247, because it is
4,941 lb. A percentage ranking would put them the other way round and bury the
one that matters.
