# COGS — measured before anything is built

Probed against the live database 2026-08-27, window **2026-08-20 … 26** (the
same window every other page in this rebuild was measured on). Dollars drift as
the Otter sync backfills; ratios hold. Re-measure before comparing a dollar.

## The finding that decides the page

**The same cost divided two ways gives 20.91% and 28.36%.**

| denominator | value | food cost |
|---|---:|---:|
| `DailyCogsItem.salesRevenue` (menu revenue, the table's own column) | $66,985 | **20.91%** |
| the statement's Total Sales (what the P&L divides by) | $49,389 | **28.36%** |

Cost is $14,008 either way. The Analytics store page already ships **27.8%**
on the statement's own COGS line ($13,745 — its rollup, not the raw sum).

So a COGS page built on the obvious column would print **20.9%** while a page
three clicks away prints **27.8%** for the same week. **Seven and a half points
apart, both labelled "food cost".** This is the same defect family as the
labour percent, the SPLH denominator and the twelve-week trend — all found and
fixed in the last two plans — and it is load-bearing here because food cost IS
this page.

**The page takes the statement's number.** One denominator across the site.

## Food cost is UNDER plan, which inverts the prototype

`Store.targetCogsPct` is **30** for Hollywood, and `targets.ts` already calls it
"the ONLY published reference in the schema". Measured weekly on the statement
basis the restaurant runs **20.7–22.6% of menu revenue**, i.e. comfortably
inside plan on either denominator.

The prototype's page is built around an overshoot — *"the red is the overshoot,
not the measure"*, a strip cell reading "N pts over plan", a table of "the items
costing the most **against plan**". Ours is under. The chart's `fillFrom: PLAN`
still works and fills the other way; the copy cannot be ported.

## The donut asks a different question than ours can answer

The prototype's donut is INGREDIENT categories — Proteins, Produce, Dry goods,
Dairy, Packaging. `DailyCogsItem.category` holds MENU categories:

```
On The Side $4,063 29.0% · NFL Promo $3,227 23.0% · Combos $3,033 21.6%
Slider and Fries Combos $1,644 11.7% · Drinks $993 7.1% · Packaging $370 2.6%
Secret Menu $366 2.6% · A La Carte $313 2.2%
```

Ingredient-category spend would have to come from the invoice line → canonical
ingredient path instead. Two different questions; pick one deliberately.

`.donut` CSS already exists (`counter-components.css:594`) and no component
renders it — a new primitive with no CSS to write.

## C-R4 settled: there is no second series to draw

The plan left "Theoretical" undecided because 60 recipes carry only 129
ingredient rows. **That worry was wrong, and the real answer is elsewhere.**

**The recipe walk is nearly complete.** In the window, of 334 lines:

| status | lines | partial | cost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `COSTED` | 327 | 15 | $14,003 |
| `MISSING_COST` | 6 | 6 | $5 |
| `UNMAPPED` | 1 | 0 | $0 |

**99.96% of the dollar is costed.** Most menu items are combos composing a few
base recipes, so 129 ingredient rows go further than the count suggests.

**But `DailyCogsItem.lineCost` IS the theoretical cost** — recipes valued at
invoice prices, multiplied by units sold. The prototype draws it against a
separate ACTUAL. Ours would have to come from purchasing, and purchasing is not
consumption without an inventory bridge:

| month | theoretical | invoiced | gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2026-03 | $54,078 | $67,633 | +25% |
| 2026-04 | $49,908 | $68,646 | +38% |
| 2026-05 | $57,171 | $49,177 | −14% |
| 2026-06 | $58,010 | $71,170 | +23% |
| 2026-07 | $65,067 | $41,168 | **−37%** |
| 2026-08 | $52,922 | $67,266 | +27% |

The gap swings from −37% to +38% and back inside six months. That is invoice
cadence — 7 invoices landed in the measured week, 34 in the month — not waste.
`StockCount` has **4 rows in the whole table**, so there is no opening/closing
stock to bridge it with.

**Drawing that as a second line would invite an owner to read July as a saving
and April as a leak, when neither happened.** One series, and the absence
declared with this table as its reason.
