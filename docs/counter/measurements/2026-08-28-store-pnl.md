# One store's P&L, measured before it was built

`P.pnlstore` (`docs/counter/counter-prototype.html`, the `P.pnlstore` block)
against the live account, 2026-08-28.

The prototype's whole subject is fixed cost: *"the fixed costs that store
actually carries and the two that carry none."* That contrast is real here.
What is not real is the flag the rest of the product uses to decide whether a
store's costs are on file.

---

## 1. Fixed costs are four columns on `Store`, and `StoreFixedExpense` is empty

| store | stage | rent | labour | cleaning | towels |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Hollywood | `ready` | **$10,390** | **$0** | $3,400 | $238.33 |
| Glendale | `pre_open` | — | — | — | — |
| Van Nuys | `pre_open` | — | — | — | — |

Hollywood carries **$14,028.33 a month**. The other two carry nothing and have
no rent on file, exactly as the prototype describes.

`StoreFixedExpense` — the model built to hold custom fixed costs, with a
label, an amount, a cadence and a GL code — holds **0 rows for every store**.
It has never been used. Every fixed cost in this account is one of the four
hard-coded columns, so the page reads those and says so rather than rendering
an empty custom-expense table.

## 2. `fixedCostsConfigured` is true on the strength of a $0 labour budget

`getAllStoresPnL` computes:

```ts
fixedCostsConfigured:
  store.fixedMonthlyLabor != null && store.fixedMonthlyRent != null
```

Hollywood's `fixedMonthlyLabor` is **0**, not null. Zero is not null, so the
flag reads **true** — for a store that pays real wages every week and has a
labour budget of nothing.

The check tests for PRESENCE and the name promises CONFIGURED. Those are
different questions, and a $0 budget answers the first while failing the
second. Anything reading this flag to decide whether a store's costs can be
trusted is being told yes.

This page does not silently fix it: `fixedCostsConfigured` is shared, other
surfaces read it, and changing its meaning is a decision with a blast radius.
What the page does is show the four inputs, mark the one that is zero, and let
a reader see the difference between "on file" and "on file and plausible".

## 3. The group statement IS this statement

Hollywood is the only trading store — `lifecycleStage = ready`; the other two
are `pre_open` with no sales, no labour and no invoices. So the group P&L and
Hollywood's P&L are the same numbers, and the page says that rather than
implying a rollup happened.

That is also why the pre-open stores are **absent** rather than shown at zero.
A zero is a reading. A missing input is not, and a store with no rent on file
would report a prime cost that is low and a margin that is high — the opposite
of the truth.

---

## What this changes about the build

1. **The fixed-cost table is the four `Store` columns**, not
   `StoreFixedExpense`, which is empty.
2. **The $0 labour budget is marked**, and the page names the gap between
   "on file" and "trustworthy" instead of repeating a flag that cannot tell
   them apart.
3. **The other two stores are named and excluded**, with the reason.
