# Invoices, measured before it was built

`P.invoices` (`docs/counter/counter-prototype.html:5621`) against the live
account (`acc_default_chrisneddys`), 2026-08-28. 226 invoices, 1,667 line
items, 2026-01-15 → 2026-08-27, all on Hollywood.

Every figure below is from `scratchpad/inv-measure.ts` … `inv4.ts`, run against
`.env.local`.

---

## 1. The reconciliation check the prototype asks for was wrong on 47 invoices

The prototype's fourth strip cell is **"Does not reconcile · 1"** and its
"Fix before approving" section is built on a gap between what the lines say and
what the invoice prints.

Run naively — `SUM(extendedPrice)` against `subtotal` — this account has **53
invoices that do not reconcile**, and 48 of them are Individual FoodService.
That reads as an extraction catastrophe on one vendor. It is not.

`I67812-00`, an IFS invoice from Aug 27:

| | |
|---|---|
| Sum of 19 extracted lines | $1,301.29 |
| Printed subtotal | $1,287.04 |
| Difference | **$14.25** |

The last two lines of that invoice are `Pallet Charge $6.50` and
`Fuel Charge $7.75`. **$6.50 + $7.75 = $14.25.** IFS prints its delivery
surcharges *below* the subtotal rule; the extractor reads them as goods. The
lines are right and the comparison was wrong.

Four product names carry these charges account-wide — `Fuel Charge` (47 rows,
$348.05), `Pallet Charge` (3, $19.50), `Miscellaneous Charge` (1, $8.95) and
`Total SALES TAX` (1, −$11.33). Excluding exactly those four from the goods
side:

| | raw | charges excluded |
|---|---|---|
| Reconciles | 173 of 226 | **219 of 226** |

47 invoices newly reconcile. One (`H87911-00`) newly breaks — its fuel charge
*is* inside the printed subtotal, so IFS is not even consistent with itself.

**The adapter reconciles on goods, not on every extracted row, and the section
says so.** A page that reported 53 broken invoices would have sent the owner
to check 47 invoices that were already correct.

`MATCHED`, so nothing has ever asked a human to look at it.

`G95788-00` is the opposite failure: $1,474.06 of goods whose extraction
produced a header and no lines at all. Also `MATCHED`.

**Both belong in the review queue and neither is in it.** The page puts them
there.

## 3. Every vendor figure is split across two spellings

Ten vendor names, six vendors:

| Invoices | Spend | Names |
|---:|---:|---|
| 53 | $114,412 | Premier Meats & Crystal Bay |
| 66 | $104,038 | Sysco |
| 52 | $52,216 | Individual FoodService |
| 24 | $51,392 | **Sysco Los Angeles, Inc.** |
| 26 | $47,052 | VITCO FOODSERVICE **\|** Vitco Foodservice |
| 2 | $7,230 | **Premier Meats** |
| 1 | $1,166 | **IFS INDIVIDUAL FOODSERVICE** |

Sysco is $155,430 across two legal names, not $104,038. Vitco's two casings are
already one row only because a `GROUP BY` on the raw string happens to place
them adjacently in a report nobody reads — grouped honestly they are the
account's fourth vendor at $47,052, not the fifth and eighth at $21,903 and
$17,087. Premier Meats is $121,642. IFS is $53,382.

This is the same defect class as the menu's item names
(`2026-08-28-catalog.md` §2): **an identity that exists only as a string the
supplier happened to print.** It is worse here, because a vendor page ranked by
spend would put Sysco second when it is first.

**The adapter groups vendors on a normalised key** — upper-cased, non-letters
stripped — and the page prints the variants it merged, so the merge is visible
rather than silent. That normalisation is a heuristic and it will not survive a
genuinely different name for the same company (`IFS INDIVIDUAL FOODSERVICE`
against `Individual FoodService` does **not** merge under it; it is listed as
its own row). Fixing that properly needs a vendor table, which is Vendors' job,
not this page's.

## 4. Line-item product names are aliased the same way, and canonicalisation already fixes it

481 distinct `productName` values across 1,667 lines. Ground beef alone:

```
GROUND BEEF FINE GRND 73/27 CREEKSTONE   10,150 lb  $45,336
GROUND BEEF FINE GRND 73/27                 906 lb   $3,956
GROUND BEEF FINE GRND                       856 lb   $3,714
Ground Beef Fine Grind 73/27 Creekstone     712 lb   $3,084
```

Four names, one product, four rows in any "top products" table keyed on the
string. **1,643 of 1,667 lines (98.6%) carry a `canonicalIngredientId`**, and
that column already unifies them.

So "What the spend was on" groups on the canonical ingredient, not on
`productName`. The prototype's own table is keyed on a product name; keyed that
way here it would put ground beef in four places and rank a $45k ingredient
below a $12k one.

## 5. The 24 unmatched lines are almost all not food

$846 across 20 distinct names, and the list is coherent: can liners (six
spellings of the same one), toilet seat covers, nitrile gloves, bagasse bowls,
register ribbon, pallet and fuel charges — and one row that is not a product at
all, `Total SALES TAX` at −$11.33, a tax line the extractor read as goods.

That is the honest content of "what these figures did not see": not a costing
gap, a **paper-and-cleaning** gap plus one mis-read tax row.

## 6. The section the prototype built for a gap that does not exist here

`P.invoices` gives half its `.split` to **"What we hold"** — 31 of 34 invoices
have a PDF, 3 predate object storage, and there is a button to refetch them.

**Every one of the 226 invoices in this account has a PDF on file.** 226 of 226,
39 of 39 in the last 30 days.

The section keeps its shape and its argument — the PDF is the only copy that
proves what a line said — and changes its subject to the custody gap that *is*
real: we hold 226 documents and read 225 of them into lines. The one we did not
is `G95788-00`.

## 7. Smaller things the page has to handle

- **14 of the last 31 days have any invoice at all.** Deliveries are not daily;
  the spend chart is mostly zeros and says "14 delivery days" rather than
  implying 31.
- **Three invoices are returns** (`isReturn`), stored with negative
  `totalAmount`, `subtotal` and `extendedPrice` throughout — so `SUM` is net
  spend with no special case, the same convention as `OtterOrder.discount`.
- **Status is not a workflow here.** All three of `REVIEW` (12), `APPROVED`
  (20) and `MATCHED` (194) carry `matchedAt` and a `matchConfidence` of ~1.0.
  `MATCHED` is what the sync writes when nothing tripped a sanity rule; it is
  the terminal state, not a step before `APPROVED`. The strip's second cell is
  labelled **"Posted to COGS"** by the prototype, which would be a claim about
  an accounting step this schema does not record — it says
  **"Matched and costed"** instead.
- **Six of the twelve `REVIEW` invoices carry structured `reviewReasons`; six
  are null** — pre-dating the field. The queue prints the reason where there is
  one and says the row predates the reasons where there is not, rather than
  inventing one.
- **Email lag is 1.3 days on average, 4.9 at worst** (109 invoices, 90 days).
  The range filter is on `invoiceDate`, so a range that ends today is still
  filling in.

## 8. The pack-shape warning is the COGS spike guard, firing correctly

Five of the six structured review reasons are `pack_shape` and four of those
are the same line on four Vitco invoices:

> Line 1 "CHRIS & EDDY'S HOUSE SCE": packSize=180 for unit=CS is implausibly
> high — likely a fused PACK/SIZE split

That is `selectNonSpikeCostIndex`'s territory (the 8× median guard). The
invoice totals are fine; the **$/unit** those lines imply is not, and it is the
house sauce — $25,224 of it in 90 days across three spellings. The queue leads
with it.
